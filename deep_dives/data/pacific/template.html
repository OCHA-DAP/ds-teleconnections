El Niño and the 2026/27 wet season in Fiji, Solomon Islands, Tonga and Vanuatu

How dry the November 2026 seasonal forecast is for the November–April wet season, how much of it comes from El Niño, and how it compares with past seasons and with CERF allocations.

Rain so far is from ERA5. CHIRPS agrees for Tonga ({{TO_chirps}}%, {{TO_chirps_rankw}} since 1981). For Fiji it is uncertain: Fiji Met's rainfall watch puts the driest August–October in the south and east, ERA5 in the north and west.

  • Fiji, Tonga and Vanuatu. SEAS5 forecasts a much drier wet season than usual, driest from November to January ({{FJ_NDJ}}%, {{TO_NDJ}}% and {{VU_NDJ}}% of the average forecast), with moderate to high skill (correlation with ERA5 of {{skill3}}).
  • Most of the forecast comes from El Niño. Compared with a neutral year, El Niño accounts for {{FJ_share}}% of the forecast shortfall in Fiji, {{TO_share}}% in Tonga and {{VU_share}}% in Vanuatu (100% meaning all of it), depending on the Niño3.4 series and on the upward trend in the forecasts. This year's Niño3.4 is beyond the 1982–2025 range, so these are extrapolations.
  • Observed rainfall follows El Niño less closely (Niño3.4 accounts for {{r2obs3_dt}}% of the year-to-year variation, against {{r2fc3_dt}}% for the forecasts). Of the four strong El Niño events before this one, 1982/83 and 1997/98 brought some of the driest wet seasons on record in Fiji and Tonga, 2015/16 was drier than average (Fiji {{FJ_o15}}%, Tonga {{TO_o15}}%) and 2023/24 wetter ({{FJ_o23}}%, {{TO_o23}}%).
  • In Solomon Islands the forecast differs by region. The south-east (Temotu, Makira-Ulawa, Rennell-Bellona) is forecast dry, like Vanuatu ({{SB_SE_NA}}%). The north-west (Choiseul, Western, Isabel) is forecast wet ({{SB_NW_NA}}%) with low skill; four strong El Niño events are too few to check this (January–March was wetter than average in {{nw_jfm_wet}}).
  • CERF's records (2006–2026) show no drought allocation to any of the four countries. The {{cerf_n}} allocations to these countries, all since 2014, were for cyclones ({{cerf_storm}}), a flood, a volcanic eruption and an earthquake. CERF's only drought allocations in the Pacific went to Papua New Guinea (2016, El Niño) and the Marshall Islands (2013). In Fiji and Tonga the 2026/27 forecast is drier than any November forecast since 1981, including those made in 1982, 1997 and 2015.

Other forecasts and official statements

__OUTLOOKS__

The national outlooks for Fiji, Tonga and Vanuatu agree with SEAS5. For Solomon Islands, the Island Climate Update is drier than SEAS5 in the north-west ({{SB_NW_NDJ}}% for November–January). Only Fiji's outlook covers February–April. Checked 17 November 2026.

Data and method

Forecast. ECMWF SEAS5 ensemble-mean monthly rainfall from the November starts 1981–2026, averaged over each country, province or division (team raster statistics). Values are percentages of the 1991–2020 average November forecast for the same months, without detrending. The seas5-skill viewer removes a linear trend first and ranks November–January to February–April 2026 as the driest on record in Fiji, Tonga and Vanuatu; without detrending some of these rank 2nd to 4th, because the forecasts have risen by {{trend3}}% per decade. Rank: among the {{n_fc}} November forecasts 1981–2026. Skill: correlation between forecast and ERA5 over 1982–2025.

Observed rainfall. ERA5 monthly rainfall over the same areas, 1981–October 2026, percentages of 1991–2020. ERA5 is a reanalysis; over small islands it largely reflects the surrounding ocean (Tonga's land covers less than one ERA5 cell). Solomon Islands groups are equal-weight averages of their provinces. Rain gauges (GHCN-Daily) were usable only in Fiji; records for Vanuatu, Tonga and Solomon Islands in that archive are too broken. For Tonga, July–October CHIRPS v2 (Climate Hazards Center) over the islands' land cells serves as a second record.

El Niño share. Straight-line fits of each area's November–April forecast (and of ERA5) on Niño3.4 known at the start of November, 1982–2025, for three series: NOAA OISST October (+{{n34_oct}} °C in 2026; 1982–2025 maximum +{{n34_oct_max}}), NOAA ERSST v6 October (+{{psl_oct}}; maximum +{{psl_oct_max}}) and the ONI for August–October (+{{oni_aso}}; maximum +{{oni_aso_max}}). Each fit is run with and without a linear trend term, since the November forecasts rise by {{trend3}}% per decade. Neutral year: the fit's value for 2026 with Niño3.4 at zero. El Niño share: the Niño3.4 term at the 2026 value as a share of the gap between the neutral year and the forecast, capped at 100%. Past forecasts scatter about the fits by {{fit_sd}} points (one standard deviation). "Accounts for x% of the variation": squared correlation after removing a linear trend from both series. OISST and ERSST differ by {{oisst_ersst_gap}} °C for October 2026, far more than in any earlier October. Niño3, Niño4 and their difference did not explain observed rainfall better than Niño3.4 (Fiji: {{r2_fj_idx}}), except in Vanuatu, where Niño4 does about as well ({{r2_vu_n4}}). October 2026 Niño4 (+{{n4_oct}} °C) is within its past range. Observed rainfall that past forecasts point to: linear fit of ERA5 on the forecast, 1982–2025, with an 80% prediction interval.

ENSO colours. October Niño3.4 (OISST): La Niña ≤ −0.5 °C, El Niño ≥ +0.5 °C, strong El Niño ≥ +1.5 °C.
